# SiteComms Australia — Finance research and coverage

**Reviewed:** 23 September 2026  
**Purpose:** Evidence register for the public finance checker and financing guide. This is not financial advice, lender accreditation, approval, or a list of SiteComms commercial partners.

## Design conclusion

The strongest Australian equipment-finance products identified are generally **national**, not state-exclusive. The state selection therefore should not manufacture separate lender lists for Queensland, NSW, Victoria, WA, SA, Tasmania, NT or ACT.

State matters most for **government schools and other public entities**, because authority to borrow, lease, procure and sign contracts can differ by jurisdiction. For private/non-government organisations, the checker uses national provider evidence and treats actual approval as a provider decision.

## National / sector evidence used by the checker

### General commercial equipment and technology finance

- **NAB — Vehicle and equipment loan**  
  https://www.nab.com.au/business/loans-and-finance/vehicle-or-equipment/vehicle-and-equipment-loan  
  Publicly describes equipment loans for business equipment including computers and fit-outs, subject to lending criteria.

- **Westpac — Vehicle and equipment finance**  
  https://www.westpac.com.au/business-banking/loans-finance/vehicle-equipment-finance/  
  Publicly describes business equipment loans, finance leases and hire purchase. Westpac states that hire-purchase ownership transfers after the final repayment.

- **CommBank — Technology equipment finance**  
  https://www.commbank.com.au/business/loans-and-finance/car-and-equipment-finance/technology-equipment-finance.html  
  Publicly lists audio-visual equipment, digital signage, projectors, smartboards, access systems, VoIP and other technology/communications equipment among lease categories.

- **BOQ — Equipment and vehicle finance**  
  https://www.boq.com.au/business/loan-and-finance/equipment-and-vehicle-finance/  
  Publicly describes finance lease, commercial hire purchase, chattel mortgage and revolving equipment-finance structures.

### Education-specific evidence

- **Vestone Capital — School equipment finance**  
  https://vestonecapital.com/school-equipment-finance/  
  Publicly describes finance for independent, Catholic and government schools and universities, including AV equipment. Structures listed include rental/operating lease, chattel mortgage and finance lease.

- **Vestone Capital — Victorian public schools**  
  https://vestonecapital.com/equipment-finance-for-victorian-public-schools/  
  Publicly describes an equipment-finance route for Victorian public schools and the associated education-sector process.

- **Finlease — Technology finance for education**  
  https://finlease.com.au/finance/technology-equipment-finance/technology-finance-education/  
  Publicly describes education technology finance and IT rental for schools and other education organisations and states it operates nationally.

### Healthcare / aged-care-adjacent evidence

- **ANZ — Health business banking**  
  https://www.anz.com.au/business/industries/health/  
  Publicly describes equipment finance and tailored business lending for the health sector. SiteComms uses this only as evidence that sector-specific finance pathways exist; it is not evidence that every residential aged-care project qualifies.

- **CommBank — Medical equipment finance**  
  https://www.commbank.com.au/business/loans-and-finance/car-and-equipment-finance/medical-equipment-finance.html  
  Publicly describes finance for healthcare equipment, technology and fit-outs, subject to asset suitability and credit approval.

## State / territory public-school governance

### Queensland

Source: Queensland Department of Education — One-to-one models  
https://education.qld.gov.au/parents-and-carers/school-information/student-device-programs/one-to-one-models

Public guidance demonstrates that lease/hire and financing structures can exist in a Queensland state-school context. The public source reviewed does **not** establish a general school-level authority to borrow for a SiteComms communications project. The checker therefore requires the school to confirm the Department's permitted finance/procurement route.

### New South Wales

Source: NSW Department of Education — Financial management  
https://education.nsw.gov.au/policy-library/policies/pd-2020-0472

The current public policy identifies school financial-management and lease-accounting procedures, but detailed school procedures are staff-only. SiteComms therefore does not infer a school-level borrowing or finance authority from public material.

### Victoria

Source: Victorian Department of Education — Finance Manual, Asset and Inventory Management  
https://www2.education.vic.gov.au/pal/asset-and-inventory-management-finance-manual-section-13/policy

The policy is unusually explicit: Victorian government schools may enter operating leases, while school councils do not have authority to borrow and cannot enter finance leases. This is encoded as a specific state result rather than a generic warning.

### Western Australia

Source: WA Department of Education — Councils and Boards in Public Schools procedures  
https://www.education.wa.edu.au/web/policies/-/councils-and-boards-in-public-schools-procedures

The public-school council/board governance framework requires a statement that the council or board will not borrow money or obtain credit. SiteComms therefore points government-school users to an approved Department/procurement route rather than treating a normal business loan as a school-controlled option.

### South Australia

Source: SA Department for Education — School Governance Administrative Instruction  
https://www.education.sa.gov.au/docs/psp/governing-council/governing-council/school-governance-administrative-instruction.pdf

The instruction states that a governing council may borrow money only with the Minister's written consent. The checker therefore treats ordinary commercial finance as requiring an authority/approval step first.

### Northern Territory

Source: NT Department of Education and Training — FARMS manual / accounting chapter  
https://education.nt.gov.au/policies/farms-finance-and-resource-management-for-schools

Current FARMS guidance distinguishes operating leases from finance leases: finance leases are borrowing and require CE approval unless a documented approved exception applies, while operating leases are not treated as borrowing. The checker preserves that distinction.

### Tasmania

Source: Ministerial Instruction No 12 for School Associations  
https://publicdocumentcentre.education.tas.gov.au/library/Document%20Centre/School-Associations-Ministerial-Instruction-No-12.pdf

School associations must obtain written Secretary approval before borrowing money and for specified restricted contracts. The checker therefore asks users to confirm the correct contracting party and approval route.

### Australian Capital Territory

Source: Procurement ACT — procurement legislation  
https://www.procurement.act.gov.au/policy-and-resources/legislation

ACT procurement legislation expressly treats acquisition by purchase, lease or rent (with or without an option to buy) as procurement by a Territory entity. The public evidence reviewed does not establish that an individual ACT public school can independently borrow or enter a particular finance contract. The checker therefore points to the ACT Government procurement route and requires Education Directorate confirmation.

## What the checker may conclude

It may say:

- a finance pathway looks realistic enough to discuss;
- a finance pathway may be available;
- a larger project is better suited to a tailored finance discussion;
- a public organisation must confirm authority/procurement before approaching a lender.

It must not say:

- finance is approved;
- a particular lender will accept the transaction;
- a stated rate, repayment or tax outcome applies;
- the user is creditworthy;
- a government school has authority to borrow merely because a lender offers finance;
- SiteComms is partnered with every cited provider.

## Maintenance rule

Provider pages and government policies are living sources. The evidence records carry a `reviewed` date and should be rechecked before a material finance-content release and during the site's normal source-governance review cycle.
